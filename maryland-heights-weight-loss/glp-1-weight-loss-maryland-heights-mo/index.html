    How GLP-1 Medications Work in Maryland Heights

    GLP-1 is a gut hormone released after meals, and these drugs copy it for days at a time instead of the few minutes the natural hormone lasts. The brain gets a steady fullness signal, and the stomach empties more slowly. Food noise often drops first. Tirzepatide adds a second hormone target called GIP, which likely explains part of why it produced larger average weight loss in head-to-head trials. The same action lowers post-meal blood sugar, which is how Ozempic and Mounjaro earned their diabetes approvals.

    Semaglutide and Tirzepatide Injections

    Both are once-weekly shots under the skin, usually in the belly or the front of the thigh. Semaglutide for weight loss is sold as Wegovy, and tirzepatide for weight loss is sold as Zepbound. Ozempic and Mounjaro are the diabetes versions of those same molecules, approved for type 2 diabetes rather than weight loss. Brand pens come prefilled. In the SURMOUNT-5 head-to-head trial, tirzepatide produced about 20 percent average weight loss at 72 weeks compared with about 14 percent on semaglutide.

    GLP-1 Pills and Oral Options in Maryland Heights

    Oral semaglutide exists as Rybelsus for type 2 diabetes, and a higher-dose oral Wegovy tablet now carries an FDA approval for weight management. The tablet goes down first thing in the morning with no more than 4 ounces of plain water, followed by at least 30 minutes before food or other pills. Skip that wait and much less of the drug gets absorbed. No needles, though. Compounded semaglutide and tirzepatide dissolving tablets are also sold, and those are not FDA-approved.

    Brand-Name vs. Compounded GLP-1s

    Wegovy and Zepbound are FDA-approved and made under federal manufacturing oversight. Compounded semaglutide and tirzepatide are mixed by licensed pharmacies for individual patients and are not FDA-approved. That means the FDA has not reviewed them for safety or effectiveness, and the agency does not check each batch the way it checks brand-name pens. Most arrive as a multi-dose vial you draw from with an insulin syringe, and some include added B12. Coverage differs too. Insurance sometimes pays for brand-name drugs with prior authorization, but compounded drugs are almost never covered.

    Dose Steps and the Weekly Schedule in Maryland Heights

    Every GLP-1 plan starts low on purpose. Wegovy begins at 0.25 mg weekly and reaches the 2.4 mg maintenance dose after about 16 weeks of monthly steps. Zepbound starts at 2.5 mg weekly and goes up by 2.5 mg every four weeks as tolerated, to a maximum of 15 mg. Nobody has to climb to the top dose, and plenty of patients stay lower when a smaller dose is working. Pick one injection day and keep it.

    What the First Three Months Feel Like

    Weeks one through four at the starting dose are mostly about tolerance, so a smaller appetite may show up before the scale moves much. Nausea tends to peak a day or two after each injection and after each dose increase. By month two, portions shrink. Most of the weight change in trials came gradually over the first 60 weeks, not the first month. Your provider reviews your weight log and side effects at each monthly check-in before approving the next dose step, and may ask what a typical lunch looks like now.

    Common Side Effects in Maryland Heights

    Nausea, constipation, diarrhea and burping are the usual complaints, mostly in the first days after starting or raising a dose. Eating smaller meals and stopping at the first sign of fullness cuts nausea for a lot of patients. Drink more water than usual. Serious risks are less common but real, including pancreatitis, gallbladder disease, kidney injury from dehydration and low blood sugar when a GLP-1 is combined with insulin or a sulfonylurea. All of these drugs carry a boxed warning about thyroid C-cell tumors seen in rodent studies.

    Who It Is For and What Gets Screened

    The usual threshold is a BMI of 30, or 27 with a weight-related condition like sleep apnea, high blood pressure, type 2 diabetes or fatty liver disease. These drugs suit adults whose weight is affecting their health after diet changes alone have stalled. Your provider asks about a personal or family history of medullary thyroid cancer or MEN2, and about any past pancreatitis or gallbladder attacks. Pregnancy is a firm no. A history of an eating disorder, or diabetic eye disease, needs a closer look before anything is prescribed.
