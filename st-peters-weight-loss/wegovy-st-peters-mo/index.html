    What Wegovy Is in St. Peters

    Wegovy is brand-name semaglutide made by Novo Nordisk and FDA-approved for chronic weight management. It comes in single-use prefilled pens that you inject once a week. No measuring is needed. Ozempic contains the same ingredient but is FDA-approved for type 2 diabetes, so Wegovy is the correct prescription when weight management is the goal. There is also an FDA-approved oral Wegovy tablet for needle-free treatment.

    Who Wegovy Is For

    Adults with a BMI of 30 or higher, or 27 or higher with a weight-related condition, are the usual candidates. Qualifying conditions include high blood pressure, high cholesterol, prediabetes and sleep apnea. Teens can qualify too. The heart risk reduction label applies to adults with heart disease and a BMI of 27 or more. It is not for type 1 diabetes, and your provider weighs every factor against your full health history before writing a prescription.

    Wegovy Dosing Schedule in St. Peters

    Wegovy starts at 0.25 mg weekly and climbs through four more monthly steps until it reaches 2.4 mg. Expect about a month on each pen strength. Pick a weekday and keep it, morning or evening, fed or fasting. The pen goes under the skin of the belly or thigh, and moving the spot each week cuts down on redness and small bumps under the skin. Five days is the window for a late dose, and after that you wait for the next scheduled shot.

    What to Expect Week by Week

    Weeks one to four at 0.25 mg are a warm-up, and some people notice only a slightly smaller appetite. The first 48 hours after each pen are when queasiness is most likely. The 1 mg step often feels different. Around month three, portion sizes tend to drop on their own. In STEP 1, weight loss kept building through about week 60 before leveling off, so the first eight weeks are only a small part of the picture.

    Wegovy Side Effects in St. Peters

    Nausea, diarrhea, constipation and vomiting are the most reported side effects, and they tend to flare in the week after a step up. Smaller meals with less grease help a lot. Hydrate well. Less common risks include pancreatitis, gallbladder disease, kidney problems from dehydration and low blood sugar when Wegovy is paired with insulin or a sulfonylurea. The label carries a boxed warning for thyroid C-cell tumors seen in rodents.

    Eligibility and Screening

    The intake asks about a personal or family history of medullary thyroid cancer or MEN2, which rules Wegovy out entirely. Your provider also looks for past pancreatitis, gallbladder disease, diabetic retinopathy, severe stomach emptying problems and any history of an eating disorder before writing a prescription. Pregnancy is a hard stop. A metabolic panel from the past year can strengthen an insurance request.
